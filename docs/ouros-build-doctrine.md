# Ouros Build Doctrine

Status: MANDATORY PRODUCTION STANDARD
Scope: every authored Minecraft/Cobblemon structure intended to survive beyond a MICRO TEST, with stricter enforcement for AUTHORED LOCATION, SIGNATURE STRUCTURE and REGIONAL SET PIECE work.

This doctrine defines how Ouros structures must be designed and reviewed from this point forward. It supplements `docs/ouros-build-quality-bar.md` and `docs/browser-build-review-gate.md`.

The project has already proved that technically valid geometry can still look amateur. Passing compilation, server boot, capture-envelope checks and floating-component checks is necessary. It is not sufficient. Architecture must also have deliberate mass, hierarchy, enclosure, material depth and professional Minecraft detailing.

## 1. Monumental massing is the default

Important Ouros structures should feel substantial.

Use broad foundations, thick walls, deep roof assemblies, layered wings, towers, buttresses, retaining masses, terraces, service volumes, vertical circulation cores and secondary structures when appropriate.

Avoid thin shells, exposed scaffolding as a dominant visual language, single-block-thick monumental walls and large buildings that read as frames with empty air between them.

A SIGNATURE STRUCTURE should normally contain a clear primary mass, secondary masses and at least one tertiary accent or landmark element.

The player should be able to identify which volume is dominant without needing signage.

Bulk must come from architectural logic. Do not inflate a structure by filling meaningless solid cubes.

## 2. No artificial height cap

There is no Ouros-specific aesthetic height limit for authored structures.

Builders may use as much vertical range as the concept needs. Towers, atria, domes, roof lanterns, cliffs, chimneys, machinery, bridges, elevated paths and arena roofs may rise far beyond earlier prototype envelopes.

Only real technical constraints apply: the active Minecraft world build limits, placement safety, performance, chunk behavior and the ability of the exact review pipeline to capture the complete structure.

Do not compress a landmark only to fit an old browser-review envelope. Expand the capture bounds when the building requires it.

Height must create hierarchy, views, interior volume, circulation or distant identity. Empty height for its own sake does not pass review.

## 3. Enclosure and weather logic

Large roofs, domes, cupolas, conservatories and canopies must look complete.

Do not leave enormous accidental openings between roof ribs, arches or structural members.

A glazed structure should normally contain actual glass or another intentional enclosure system between its structural ribs.

A civic or inhabited building should have believable protection from rain and weather unless the space is deliberately exterior.

Open-air architecture is allowed when the fiction and gameplay need it: courtyards, amphitheaters, ruins, pergolas, garden walks, loading areas, observation decks and similar spaces. The openness must read as intentional architecture, not unfinished construction.

Transitions between enclosed and open space need thresholds, eaves, arcades, vestibules, gates, drainage or other spatial cues.

## 4. Roofs are architecture

A major roof cannot be a flat plate placed over finished walls.

Use compound roof forms, ridges, valleys, hips, gables, dormers, clerestories, gutters, downspouts, eaves, fascia, parapets, roof gardens, ventilation, skylights or other appropriate systems.

Roofs must have a visible load path. Large eaves should connect to columns, walls, trusses, brackets or cantilever logic.

Avoid floating roof fragments and decorative caps with no physical relationship to the building below.

Avoid repetitive stair-step patterns when they read as an algorithm rather than authored architecture.

Use stairs, slabs, walls, trapdoors and oriented logs deliberately to refine pitch, thickness, trim and joinery.

## 5. Professional facade depth

Important facades should work at three viewing distances.

From far away: silhouette, mass hierarchy and roof profile.

From medium distance: bays, setbacks, balconies, frames, columns, window rhythm, structural changes and material zones.

From close range: sills, lintels, shutters, brackets, railings, signs, planters, lamps, drainage, vents, joints, wear, furnishings and other human-scale details.

A facade that is visually finished only from one distance does not pass.

Avoid long uninterrupted planes. Break them using structure and function rather than random noise.

Windows need frames, recesses, sills, lintels or another depth treatment appropriate to the style.

Entrances need hierarchy. A major entrance should be identifiable through massing, approach, light and detailing before text labels are considered.

## 6. Decorative blocks are structural vocabulary

Do not build showcase locations almost entirely from full cubes.

Use appropriate vanilla decorative and shape blocks as part of the architectural language: stairs, slabs, walls, fences, fence gates, trapdoors, doors, signs, hanging signs, chains, lanterns, candles, panes, bars, grates, lightning rods, buttons, pressure plates, carpets, banners, pots, planters, leaves, roots, shelves, barrels, cauldrons, composters, scaffolding used intentionally, workstations and other functional props.

Decorative blocks must support the fiction of the room or facade. Random clutter does not count as detail.

When approved modded decorative blocks become available through the production smoke set and exact viewer pipeline, they may extend this vocabulary. A modded palette must remain coherent and dependency-safe.

## 7. Material palettes need hierarchy and variation

Every authored structure needs an intentional material palette.

Define at minimum:

- foundation/base material family;
- primary wall or structural family;
- roof family;
- trim/accent family;
- glazing/opening treatment;
- landscape/vegetation family;
- interior/service material family where applicable.

Use texture variation inside those families through related blocks, weathering, masonry transitions, stripped logs, copper oxidation, moss, cracked variants or other controlled variation.

Do not create visual noise by using many unrelated blocks simply to increase palette count.

Material changes should communicate age, function, exposure, repair, hierarchy or construction method.

## 8. Advanced Minecraft building techniques are expected

SIGNATURE STRUCTURE work should deliberately use advanced composition techniques where appropriate.

Examples include:

- layered wall depth and double-wall construction;
- recessed and projected facade bays;
- supported cantilevers;
- compound roofs and secondary roof volumes;
- arches and vault-like profiles built with stairs/slabs/walls;
- buttresses and structural piers;
- cornices and multi-layer eaves;
- dormers, clerestories and roof lanterns;
- diagonal or curved visual forms approximated through controlled voxel stepping;
- custom tree branching and canopy masses;
- retaining systems and stepped foundations;
- integrated bridges and elevated circulation;
- custom window assemblies;
- gradients and controlled texturing;
- negative-space composition;
- asymmetry used intentionally to break procedural repetition;
- interior ceiling structure rather than exposed roof undersides;
- lighting integrated into architecture rather than torches placed afterward.

A technique is valuable only when it improves the design. Do not use complexity as decoration without purpose.

## 9. Structural support must read visually

The automated floating-component audit remains mandatory, but visual support is stricter than simple block connectivity.

A roof can be technically connected and still look unsupported.

Large masses need believable columns, walls, trusses, arches, buttresses, foundations or suspended systems.

Bridges need abutments or suspension/support logic.

Balconies need brackets, beams, walls or cantilever depth.

Hanging lights need chains or mounting hardware that visibly reach a support.

Large tree branches need trunks, forks and branch taper that visually explain their load path.

Do not exploit hidden one-block connections merely to satisfy the automated audit.

## 10. Interiors need ceilings, thickness and occupation

Important interiors cannot be empty volumes inside attractive shells.

Interior walls need thickness or believable framing where visible.

Ceilings need authored structure, panels, beams, vaults, suspended systems, skylights or another deliberate treatment.

Rooms need functional furniture and props tied to their use.

Public, staff, service, storage and mechanical spaces should read differently.

Large halls need subdivisions, overlooks, balconies, planting, furnishings, circulation or focal elements so that they do not feel like empty boxes.

Back-of-house architecture is part of the build, not leftover space.

## 11. Arena and challenge-space doctrine

A Gym arena must be recognizable as a major institutional climax.

It needs a deliberate battle floor, boundary language, entrances for participants, leader staging, spectator or observation logic, staff/officiating positions, lighting, vertical volume and backstage/service access where appropriate.

The arena should have one or more signature visual elements tied to the Gym identity.

Do not approve a rectangle with a border, a flat decorative pattern or an empty hall as a finished leader arena.

Challenge rooms must have spatial identity beyond obstacle placement. Architecture, puzzle mechanisms, circulation and environmental storytelling should reinforce each other.

## 12. Terrain and landscape are part of the architecture

Do not place major builds on obvious square grass plates in production.

Approach terrain should transition into foundations using grading, retaining walls, stairs, ramps, drainage, vegetation, rocks, water, paths or other deliberate landscape systems.

Large trees need authored species/age/role variation. Avoid repeating one procedural canopy shape around a landmark.

Ground detailing should include meaningful surface variation and edges. Grass, dirt, paths, stone, water, planting beds, service surfaces and erosion should reflect use.

Landscape must support distant composition and human-scale exploration.

## 13. Signature detail density

Every major authored view should contain detail at multiple scales without becoming cluttered.

For SIGNATURE STRUCTURE review, inspect representative 8x8 to 12x12 block facade/interior patches. A patch containing only a wall plane, one window and one full-block material is usually under-detailed unless the style deliberately demands minimalism.

Look for combinations of structure, trim, opening treatment, lighting, props, vegetation, signage, wear or functional hardware.

Repeated modules need controlled variation so the player can perceive authorship rather than copy-paste repetition.

## 14. Build from authored modules, not endless correction passes

A weak base form should be replaced rather than buried under decorative passes.

Detailed rooms, facade modules, roof pieces and service elements should increasingly move into StructureTemplate/NBT assets or reusable authored modules when that improves iteration quality.

Java geometry remains valuable for curves, organic forms, terrain adaptation, stateful transformations and connective systems.

Do not allow an accumulation of correction passes to become the permanent architecture pipeline for a signature structure.

## 15. Browser review must show the whole building

The exact Minecraft viewer must include the complete authored geometry.

A build cannot pass visual review if its roof, tower, basement, terrain transition or service wing is clipped by capture bounds.

The review envelope must expand with the building. Height and footprint statistics must come from the actual exported geometry.

Review at minimum:

- distant approach / three-quarter silhouette;
- opposite or service-side three-quarter view;
- high view showing roof composition;
- ground/cutaway view showing circulation and interiors;
- arena or primary destination;
- representative close-detail view when the viewer supports it.

## Mandatory pre-approval checklist

A SIGNATURE STRUCTURE or REGIONAL SET PIECE cannot be called finished until every applicable item below is explicitly satisfied.

### Mass and silhouette

- [ ] The build is substantial enough for its narrative importance.
- [ ] Primary, secondary and tertiary masses are readable.
- [ ] The silhouette is recognizable from at least one distant approach.
- [ ] Height was chosen for the concept, not constrained by an obsolete project envelope.
- [ ] Tall elements create hierarchy or usable space rather than empty vertical filler.

### Roof and enclosure

- [ ] Major roofs have thickness, structure, edges and drainage/termination logic.
- [ ] Roof masses visibly connect to their supporting architecture.
- [ ] Glazed domes/canopies/clerestories contain intentional glazing or enclosure.
- [ ] Large accidental open gaps are absent.
- [ ] Open-air zones are clearly intentional and spatially finished.

### Facades and materials

- [ ] Major facades have far, medium and close-range detail.
- [ ] Long flat walls are broken by functional architectural depth.
- [ ] Windows and doors have authored frames/recesses/trim.
- [ ] The material palette has clear hierarchy.
- [ ] Texture variation is controlled rather than random.

### Decoration and occupation

- [ ] The build uses shape/decorative blocks beyond full cubes where appropriate.
- [ ] Lighting is integrated into architecture.
- [ ] Props explain room and service functions.
- [ ] Repeated modules contain authored variation.
- [ ] Major spaces contain close-range detail without random clutter.

### Structure and interiors

- [ ] Automated floating-component audit passes.
- [ ] Visual load paths are believable even where automation would accept a hidden connection.
- [ ] Balconies, bridges, roofs and hanging elements have visible supports.
- [ ] Important interiors have authored ceilings.
- [ ] Public, staff and service circulation is spatially believable.

### Arena / gameplay destination

- [ ] The primary gameplay space has a strong identity.
- [ ] A Gym arena reads as an institutional battle venue immediately.
- [ ] Participant, leader, spectator/staff and service access are accounted for where applicable.
- [ ] Puzzle/challenge architecture has more identity than obstacle placement alone.

### Terrain and vegetation

- [ ] No obvious production grass plate or giant flattened rectangle remains.
- [ ] Foundations meet terrain intentionally.
- [ ] Paths, drainage and service access connect logically.
- [ ] Important trees/vegetation have authored variation and believable forms.
- [ ] Landscape composition supports the building silhouette.

### Review and technical evidence

- [ ] Complete geometry fits inside the exact browser capture.
- [ ] Capture-envelope audit passes.
- [ ] Server boot and production smoke checks pass.
- [ ] Exact BlockState export succeeds.
- [ ] Browser review includes silhouette, roof, ground/cutaway and primary-destination views.
- [ ] Known visual weaknesses are recorded instead of being hidden behind the word "finished".

## Automatic visual rejection conditions

A signature candidate must return to implementation when any of the following dominate the review:

- a skinny exposed frame or scaffolding silhouette where a substantial building is expected;
- a large box with decoration pasted onto its surfaces;
- giant roof objects that appear balanced above unrelated lower masses;
- roofs or cupolas that look unfinished because large enclosure areas are missing;
- repeated stair-step diagonals that read as crude algorithmic geometry;
- a forest of identical posts with no structural hierarchy;
- large flat walls without secondary depth;
- an arena that reads as a simple floor pattern or generic hall;
- trees that look like repeated procedural blobs;
- an obvious rectangular terrain plate around the build;
- decorative clutter without functional or narrative logic;
- a technically connected element that still looks visibly unsupported;
- capture bounds that hide or crop important geometry.

## Exceptions

Not every Ouros structure must be huge. Homes, sheds, route shelters, kiosks, minor ruins and utility buildings should use scale appropriate to their role.

The doctrine is about quality and hierarchy. Small authored structures still need convincing proportions, roofs, material logic, close detail, terrain integration and occupation.

Minimalist architecture is allowed when intentionally authored. Minimalism does not excuse placeholder geometry.

Ruins and damaged buildings may expose structure, missing roof sections or open walls when that condition is authored history and the remaining geometry still reads as deliberately composed.

## Current consequence for Meridian Canopy Gym

Meridian remains a SIGNATURE STRUCTURE candidate under active implementation.

The current rebuild is a valid prototype base, but it is not automatically approved by structural CI. Future Meridian passes should prefer heavier integrated masses, complete glazed roof systems, stronger arena enclosure, richer facade/interior detailing, more landscape integration and fewer exposed structural frames.
